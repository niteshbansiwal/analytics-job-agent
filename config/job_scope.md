# 4. MOST IMPORTANT: LINKEDIN HIRING-POST HUNT

Do not restrict the search to LinkedIn Jobs.

I specifically want you to search ordinary LinkedIn posts written by people who are hiring or sharing openings on behalf of their colleagues, friends, managers or companies.

These posts are often more useful than formal job listings because they reveal the actual person behind the vacancy.

### Search phrases

Search LinkedIn and publicly indexed LinkedIn content using phrases such as:

- "Hiring Analytics Manager"
- "We are hiring Analytics Manager"
- "Hiring for Analytics Manager"
- "Looking for an Analytics Manager"
- "Hiring Business Analytics Manager"
- "Manager Analytics – Hiring"
- "Senior Manager Analytics – Hiring"
- "Hiring Data Analytics Manager"
- "Analytics leadership opportunity"
- "My team is hiring Analytics Manager"
- "A friend of mine is hiring Analytics Manager"
- "One of my colleagues is looking for an Analytics Manager"
- "Urgent hiring – Analytics Manager"
- "Refer candidates for Analytics Manager"

Also search combinations of:
- Exact job title + company.
- Job title + hiring.
- Job title + city.
- Analytics + recruiter.
- Analytics + Talent Acquisition.
- Analytics + "my team".
- Analytics + "share your resume".

Search for variations in wording, not just one exact phrase.

### LinkedIn search approach

1. Search public LinkedIn posts for the hiring phrases.
2. Identify posts that actually advertise a vacancy.
3. Open the post and capture its original author.
4. Determine whether the author is:
   - The hiring manager.
   - The recruiter or TA partner.
   - An employee sharing their company's vacancy.
   - A friend or colleague sharing an opening on behalf of someone else.
5. Navigate to the author's LinkedIn profile.
6. Verify their name, current designation and company.
7. Capture the direct post URL and profile URL.
8. Extract the job details and compare them against my resume.

Do not confuse the author of a post with the actual hiring manager.

If an employee says "my friend is hiring", identify the author and, if publicly identifiable, the actual hiring person separately.

If the LinkedIn post contains a job application email, capture it only if it is explicitly published and appears to be a genuine professional contact.

Do not guess email addresses.
